# Creative Technologist — Take-Home Exercise

Thanks for making it to this stage. This exercise is designed to give you space to show how you think, build, and use AI as a co-pilot. It should take **about 4 hours**, with a **6-hour hard cap** — please don't go longer. We'd rather see what you make in 4 focused hours than a polished week-long project.

If you have any questions before you start, just reply to the email you received this in.

---

## The prompt

**Build a small, data-driven creative web experience.**

We've attached three JSON datasets (~100 records each). Pick one — whichever sparks something for you:

1. **US lighthouses** (`us-lighthouses.json`) — name, state, year first lit, structure type, height, active vs. deactivated, coordinates
2. **Nuclear detonations** (`nuclear-detonations.json`) — name, country, year, yield in kilotons, test type, site, coordinates
3. **Endangered languages** (`endangered-languages.json`) — name, language family, country, speaker count, endangerment status, coordinates

Design and build a **single-page interactive experience** around the dataset you choose. Make us feel something about the data — surprise us, move us, or make us see it differently. The "angle" you find in the data is half the exercise.

---

## Constraints

- **Stack:** Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS or any framework you prefer. Your call.
- **Motion:** At least one interaction must use [GSAP](https://gsap.com/). We want to see your sense of motion.
- **Responsive:** It must work on mobile. We will open it on a phone.
- **Deployed:** Ship it to a public URL (Vercel, Netlify, GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages — anywhere).
- **AI is required, not optional.** Use Cursor and any AI tools you like. We want to see how you direct AI, not the other way around.

---

## Deliverables

Send us four things:

1. **Deployed URL** — the live experience
2. **GitHub repo** — public or share access with the email on your invite
3. **A 3–5 minute Loom** walking us through:
   - Your concept and the angle you found in the data
   - One design trade-off you made and why
   - **Your AI workflow** — show 2–3 actual prompts (wins *and* misses). What did you ask AI to do, and what did you decide to do yourself?
   - One thing you'd improve with another day
4. **A one-page README** in the repo: approach, what you cut, how to run locally

> The Loom is the most important piece. A great build with no Loom is harder for us to evaluate than a rougher build with a clear, confident walkthrough.

---

## What we're looking for

- **Design craft** — typography, layout, motion, hierarchy, restraint. Not "more features." Better choices.
- **Technical execution** — works on mobile, no console errors, clean repo, deploys cleanly.
- **AI workflow** — did you steer the AI, or did the AI steer you? We're looking for prompt and context engineering, not just "I asked ChatGPT and pasted the output."
- **Conceptual angle** — did you find something interesting in the data, or just visualize it?
- **Communication** — clarity in the Loom and README. If we hired you, this is how you'd hand off work.

There is no single right answer. A minimal, beautifully crafted page can beat a feature-packed one. A weird, opinionated angle can beat a "safe" one.

---

## Ground rules

- **Use any AI tools you want.** Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, v0, Midjourney, whatever. Just be honest about what you used in the Loom.
- **Don't ship someone else's work.** Components, libraries, and AI-generated code are fine and expected. Wholesale-copying another portfolio piece is not.
- **Stop at 6 hours.** If you're not done, ship what you have and tell us what you cut in the README. Knowing where to stop is part of the job.
- **Ask questions.** If the prompt is ambiguous, that's intentional — but if something is genuinely blocking you, email us. We won't penalize good questions.

We're excited to see what you make. Have fun with it.
